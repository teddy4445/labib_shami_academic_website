Biography

Labib Shami is an economist and a Senior Researcher at the Taub Center for Social Policy Studies in Israel, and a faculty member in the Department of Economics at Western Galilee College. He has also taught in the Department of Economics at the University of Haifa.

He received his PhD in Economics from the University of Haifa in 2018. His doctoral thesis, Dynamic Monetary Equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s Price Mechanism, was supervised by Anna Rubinchik and Nissim Cohen, and shares its title with his 2019 article in the Journal of Macroeconomics. He studied economics at graduate level at the University of Haifa and the Technion, after a first degree in economics at the Open University of Israel.

His research spans macroeconomics and monetary policy, the non-observed economy and tax evasion, credit markets, small and medium-sized enterprises and local government, and the economics of crises. Much of his recent work combines economic theory with computational methods, including machine learning and agent-based simulation, developed with collaborators in applied mathematics and computer science.

At the Taub Center he analyses Israel’s macroeconomy, prices, housing and household finance. He has discussed this work in Arabic-, Hebrew- and English-language media.

Longer biography

Dr. Labib Shami is an economist whose work links economic theory, computational modelling and policy analysis. He is a Senior Researcher at the Taub Center for Social Policy Studies in Israel and a faculty member in the Department of Economics at Western Galilee College, and has taught at the University of Haifa, where he received his PhD in Economics in 2018. His research addresses monetary policy and inflation, credit markets and household finance, the non-observed economy and tax evasion, small and medium-sized enterprises and local government, and the economics of crises. At the Taub Center he co-authors the macroeconomic chapter of the annual State of the Nation Report and studies prices, housing and household debt. His articles have appeared in journals including the Journal of Macroeconomics, the European Journal of Political Economy, Computational Economics and Local Government Studies.

Research

Research & publications

Labib Shami’s research connects economic theory, computational methods and policy-relevant evidence. A question from his doctoral thesis, how monetary policy interacts with economic activity that official statistics do not record, runs through much of the work, which has since broadened to credit markets, small business and local government, and the economics of crises.

01 Themes

Questions, methods and evidence

Money, credit & households

How do monetary policy, cash and credit markets shape the opportunities and risks facing households?

Approach. Dynamic general-equilibrium models with inside and outside money and heterogeneous agents; models of competition in credit markets; simulations of mortgage payments under different paths of interest rates and inflation.

Why it matters. Interest rates, inflation and access to credit are where macroeconomic policy meets household budgets.

The non‑observed economy

How large is the part of the economy that official statistics do not record, and what makes it grow or shrink?

Approach. Currency-demand estimation adapted to Israel; machine-learning estimators; spatio-temporal models across regions; agent-based simulations of tax compliance and tax revenue.

Why it matters. Unrecorded activity affects tax revenue, the reliability of official statistics and the case for policies such as limits on the use of cash.

Policy, enterprise & resilience

Which public decisions help local economies, small businesses and whole countries absorb shocks?

Approach. Survey-based structural equation models of SMEs and municipalities; case studies of entrepreneurship programmes; coupled epidemiological and economic models; game-theoretic and dynamic models of public goods and interest groups.

Why it matters. Many consequential economic decisions are taken locally, or under crisis conditions, often with limited evidence.

02 Selected work

Six papers to start with

An editorial selection across the three themes, not a ranking.

  • 2019

    Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanism

    Labib Shami · Journal of Macroeconomics

    A general-equilibrium model with inside and outside money, tax-evading households, a government and a central bank. It shows how inflation, as a monetary-policy instrument, interacts dynamically with the size of the non-observed economy, and uses Shapley and Shubik's price mechanism to establish a monetary equilibrium in which formal and informal markets coexist.

    Summary: Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanismDOI: Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanism

  • 2020

    Towards a cashless economy: Economic and socio-political implications

    Nissim Cohen, Anna Rubinchik and Labib Shami · European Journal of Political Economy

    A general-equilibrium model of an economy with official and informal sectors, showing how eliminating cash can misallocate resources and reduce the gains from trade, alongside other unintended consequences of abolishing cash.

    Summary: Towards a cashless economy: Economic and socio-political implicationsDOI: Towards a cashless economy: Economic and socio-political implications

  • 2024

    Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed Economy

    Labib Shami and Teddy Lazebnik · Computational Economics

    Shows that machine-learning methods, notably a random forest, estimate currency demand more accurately than the standard currency-demand regressions, improving estimates of the size of unregistered economic activity.

    Summary: Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed EconomyDOI: Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed Economy

  • 2026

    Tell Me Who Your Neighbors Are and I Will Tell You Your Informal Economy Size: The Case of Sweden

    Teddy Lazebnik, Labib Shami and Einav Peretz-Andersson · Computational Economics Online firstOpen access

    Brings geography into informal-economy estimation. Using data for Sweden's 21 regions from 2006 to 2023, the study compares models ranging from linear regression to graph neural networks for predicting the size of each region's informal economy under different spatio-temporal settings.

    Summary: Tell Me Who Your Neighbors Are and I Will Tell You Your Informal Economy Size: The Case of SwedenDOI: Tell Me Who Your Neighbors Are and I Will Tell You Your Informal Economy Size: The Case of Sweden

  • 2025

    Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competition

    Labib Shami · Journal of Banking Regulation

    Models what happens when newly established non-bank lenders that depend on bank financing enter a credit market. Because borrowers' insolvency risk plays a central role, more credit providers can raise rather than lower average credit prices, which argues for equal conditions for new entrants.

    Summary: Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competitionDOI: Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competition

  • 2025

    Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performance

    Israel Rachevski and Labib Shami · Local Government Studies

    Links the local business environment created by municipalities to SME owners' satisfaction and to local economic outcomes. Factor analysis and structural equation modelling on a survey of 513 SME owners show that higher satisfaction is associated with more local employment and higher municipal tax revenue from SMEs.

    Summary: Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performanceDOI: Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performance

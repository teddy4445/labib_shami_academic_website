Academic research initiative

Israel Tax Research Lab

Research on taxation, compliance, and the non-observed economy in Israel.

01 About the Lab

Understanding how taxation shapes the economy

The Israel Tax Research Lab is an academic research initiative in development. It aims to bring together early-career researchers, doctoral candidates, and master’s students to study Israel’s tax environment and the effects of taxation on economic activity, public revenues, and society.

The Lab’s work will combine rigorous academic research with questions that matter to policy and practice. It will examine how tax systems are designed and administered, how individuals and businesses respond to them, and what drives tax non-compliance and economic activity that is not fully captured in official statistics.

02 Research agenda

Areas of focus

The Lab’s research agenda will cover the institutions, incentives, and economic activity connected to taxation.

Tax policy and its effects

How tax design affects households, businesses, employment, investment, inequality, and public revenue.

Compliance and tax evasion

Why people and firms comply with tax rules or evade them, and how incentives, norms, and enforcement shape those choices.

Tax administration and enforcement

How tax authorities measure compliance, address the tax gap, and design effective and proportionate enforcement.

The non-observed economy

How to measure economic activity that is not fully recorded, understand its drivers, and assess its fiscal and wider economic implications.

Money laundering and illicit financial flows

Connections between financial opacity, tax-related offences, money laundering, and the movement of illicit funds.

04 A collaborative academic community

Research across disciplines and career stages

The Lab is being developed as a collaborative academic platform for researchers at different career stages, including early-career researchers, doctoral candidates, and master’s students. Its planned activities include collaborative research, working papers, seminars, and policy-focused knowledge exchange.

Researchers and students interested in this agenda are welcome to get in touch to discuss potential participation.

05 Knowledge exchange

Developing research partnerships

Discussions are under way to establish research collaborations with the Israel Tax Authority and the Budget Department of Israel’s Ministry of Finance. These collaborations are subject to formal agreements; further details will be added as arrangements are concluded.

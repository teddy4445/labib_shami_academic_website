01 Research

Three lines of inquiry

The same questions run through the theoretical papers, the computational models and the policy reports.

The non‑observed economy

How large is the economy that official statistics miss, and what drives it?

Measuring and modelling non-observed economic activity and tax evasion, from currency-demand estimates to machine learning, spatial models and agent-based simulation.

  • Article · 2026The non-observed economy of Sweden’s regions
  • Article · 2024Machine learning and the size of the non-observed economy
  • Policy · 2020The Non-Observed Economy in Israel

Money, credit & households

How do monetary policy and credit markets reach household budgets?

General-equilibrium models of money and inflation, the economics of cash, competition among lenders, and applied work on mortgages, household debt and the cost of living.

  • Article · 2025Elusive competition: non-bank credit providers
  • Policy · 2023Interest, Inflation, and Mortgages in Israel
  • Article · 2019Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a non-observed economy

Policy, enterprise & resilience

Which public decisions help economies, and the businesses in them, absorb shocks?

Small and medium-sized enterprises and local government, entrepreneurship, public goods and interest groups, and the economics of crises, from pandemics to wartime disruption.

  • Article · 2025SME owners’ satisfaction and local government revenue

02 Selected research

From monetary theory to local policy

All publications

  • 2025

    Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competition

    Labib Shami · Journal of Banking Regulation

    Models what happens when newly established non-bank lenders that depend on bank financing enter a credit market. Because borrowers' insolvency risk plays a central role, more credit providers can raise rather than lower average credit prices, which argues for equal conditions for new entrants.

    Summary: Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competitionDOI: Elusive competition: impact of non-bank credit providers’ financing on credit market competition

  • 2025

    Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performance

    Israel Rachevski and Labib Shami · Local Government Studies

    Links the local business environment created by municipalities to SME owners' satisfaction and to local economic outcomes. Factor analysis and structural equation modelling on a survey of 513 SME owners show that higher satisfaction is associated with more local employment and higher municipal tax revenue from SMEs.

    Summary: Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performanceDOI: Twice as effective: business environment, SME owners’ satisfaction, and local government revenue performance

  • 2024

    Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed Economy

    Labib Shami and Teddy Lazebnik · Computational Economics

    Shows that machine-learning methods, notably a random forest, estimate currency demand more accurately than the standard currency-demand regressions, improving estimates of the size of unregistered economic activity.

    Summary: Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed EconomyDOI: Implementing Machine Learning Methods in Estimating the Size of the Non-observed Economy

  • 2020

    Towards a cashless economy: Economic and socio-political implications

    Nissim Cohen, Anna Rubinchik and Labib Shami · European Journal of Political Economy

    Develops a general-equilibrium model showing how eliminating cash can distort trade between observed and non-observed sectors, reduce gains from trade and create wider economic and socio-political consequences.

    DOI: Towards a cashless economy: Economic and socio-political implications

  • 2019

    Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanism

    Labib Shami · Journal of Macroeconomics

    A general-equilibrium model with inside and outside money, tax-evading households, a government and a central bank. It shows how inflation, as a monetary-policy instrument, interacts dynamically with the size of the non-observed economy, and uses Shapley and Shubik's price mechanism to establish a monetary equilibrium in which formal and informal markets coexist.

    Summary: Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanismDOI: Dynamic monetary equilibrium with a Non-Observed Economy and Shapley and Shubik’s price mechanism

03 Policy analysis

Evidence for public debate

At the Taub Center, Dr. Labib Shami studies prices, housing and household finance. Since 2021, he has co-authored the macroeconomic chapter of the Center’s annual State of the Nation Report with Prof. Benjamin Bental.

All policy reports

16 September 2026

The Consumer Price Index, Housing Costs, Consumer Credit, and the Cost of Living

Benjamin Bental and Labib Shami

Argues that Israel's consumer price index understates the cost pressure on households because it leaves out mortgage and consumer-credit interest, constructs an alternative finance-based housing measure, and recommends publishing supplementary indices alongside the official one.

Taub Center pagePDF

29 November 2023

Interest, Inflation, and Mortgages in Israel

Labib Shami, Ori Oberman and Benjamin Bental

Simulates how Bank of Israel rate increases, compared with inflation left unchecked, would change households' monthly mortgage payments, and concludes that unchecked inflation would hurt borrowers more than the rate increases.

Taub Center pagePDF

10 September 2020

The Non-Observed Economy in Israel

Labib Shami

Estimates the size of Israel's non-observed economy with a currency-demand model, tracing its recent decline, separating its tax-evasion and crime components, and considering the possible effects of limits on cash and of COVID-19.

Taub Center pagePDF
